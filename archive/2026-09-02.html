Vendor details
Datadog Active

No product or partnership news, but a significant press item. The Information reported that Palo Alto Networks CEO Nikesh Arora held acquisition discussions with both Okta and Datadog over the past 18 months. Arora met Olivier Pomel in spring 2025 and pitched a hypothetical deal when Datadog was publicly valued above $40B; Pomel was unreceptive and no formal offer was made. Datadog's market cap has since roughly doubled to about $80B, which most commentary reads as putting a deal out of reach. Palo Alto's fallback moves are the more useful signal: CyberArk in July 2025, and $3.35B for Chronosphere — a direct Datadog rival in logs and observability — in January 2026.

DDOG closed the window near $224 after a roughly 5% pop on the report, accompanied by a Buy upgrade at a $300 target citing AI observability demand.

Watch signal: When a security platform is willing to buy observability outright, the category's strategic value is being repriced upward — which strengthens Elastic's single-platform search, security and observability story, but also raises the odds of further consolidation among Elastic's competitors.

Standing items: management presents at Citi Global TMT on 8 September and Goldman Sachs Communacopia on 10 September — the next public read on Q3 spending trends after the post-Q2 drop on 6 August. DASH 2026's 100+ capabilities, led by the autonomous Bits AI family, remain the product baseline.

Grafana Labs Active

Grafana Labs open-sourced the Grafana AI SDK for Go, a shared foundation it originally built for its own backend teams. It provides common interfaces for calling models, streaming responses, exposing typed Go functions as tools, producing schema-validated structured output, and running multi-step agents with human-approval gates, timeouts, retries and model fallback. It speaks the Vercel AI SDK protocol, so a Go backend can stream straight into useChat, useCompletion and useObject, and it supports Grafana Cloud, Anthropic, OpenAI, Amazon Bedrock and any OpenAI-compatible endpoint.

Alongside it, Grafana published anomaly detection, outlier detection and forecasting in Grafana Cloud, a knowledge-graph-as-LLM-context piece on root cause analysis, Session Replay in Frontend Observability, and production guidance on running Alloy as a central telemetry gateway. Grafana 13.2 (saved queries GA, view-panel controls sidebar, Git Sync for GitHub Enterprise) is the current release, and the company is carrying its 2026 Gartner Magic Quadrant Leader placement across the site. Reported ARR is around $605M as of August.

Watch signal: Giving away agent-building primitives buys developer mindshare cheaply, so Elastic's edge needs to rest on retrieval quality, index economics and data-tier control rather than on agent tooling parity.

ClickHouse Quiet

No product, partnership or press development in the window; the last press release was ClickHouse Labs under Andy Pavlo on 3 August. Standing items worth tracking: the Open House roadshow resumes in New York on 10 September, Bengaluru on 22 September with the Click-a-Thon finals, then Singapore and London later in the month. Reported ARR is around $350M as of August against $160M at the end of 2025, on a $15B Series D valuation with stated IPO intent. ClickStack Cloud remains in limited private preview on AWS us-east-1, and the open-source ClickStack MCP server continues to be the vehicle for positioning ClickHouse as the store behind AI SRE agents.

Dynatrace Active

Two items landed in trade press on 31 August. CRN examined the Arize acquisition — signed 13 August, $915M in cash and stock with roughly $815M cash, expected to close this quarter or early next, subject to regulatory review. Arize is a 200-person Berkeley company founded in 2020 specialising in AI observability and evaluation; founders Jason Lopatecki and Aparna Dhinakaran both join at closing, with Lopatecki reporting to Rick McConnell. The strategic logic is joining Arize's visibility into model outputs, agent trajectories and tool use to Dynatrace's application, service and infrastructure telemetry.

Separately, the State of SRE and Platform Engineering 2026 report drew pickup: a survey of 919 IT leaders finding that 73% of SRE and platform engineering teams now share responsibilities across reliability and platform domains, 67% of SREs name AI model monitoring their top use case, and 58% already run model performance and accuracy monitoring. Dynatrace cites Gartner's projection that 80% of enterprises will adopt SRE practices by 2028, up from 30% in 2024.

Watch signal: Dynatrace is assembling end-to-end AI-lifecycle observability by acquisition, so Elastic should expect model evaluation, agent trajectory tracing and guardrail reporting to become standard RFP line items rather than differentiators.

New Relic Active

New Relic's Japan region went generally available on 31 August, delivering on the March commitment to open a Japanese data centre. The region is hosted in AWS Tokyo, with observability data ingested, stored and processed inside Japan, and product documentation updated across the portfolio. The most consequential detail is that AI processing for Japan-region customers runs in-region: because an investigating agent reads telemetry metadata and payloads, inference outside the country would reintroduce exactly the cross-border flow the region exists to eliminate. New Relic is targeting financial services, telecommunications, healthcare, public infrastructure and their manufacturing suppliers.

Watch signal: In-region inference — not just in-region storage — is becoming the sovereignty bar, so Elastic's APJ conversations should establish region coverage and where model inference physically executes before the competitive comparison starts.

Splunk / Cisco Quiet

No announcements in the window — most likely pre-conference embargo. .conf26 runs 14–17 September at the Colorado Convention Center in Denver with roughly 6,500 attendees expected, framed around the agentic era, with Splunk University from 12 September and BOTS 11 returning. Standing items: Cisco Data Fabric built on Splunk Enterprise and Splunk Cloud; Splunk Agent Observability powered by Cisco's Galileo acquisition, with pre-built toxicity, bias and hate-speech evaluations plus production tracing and runtime guardrails; and AI Agent Monitoring landing in Observability Cloud and AppDynamics with a Cisco AI Defense integration for prompt injection and data leakage. Expect the next material Splunk news on 14 September.
